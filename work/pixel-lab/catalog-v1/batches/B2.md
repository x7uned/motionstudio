## meter-bar (prop, HUD) · agent B2b
- Source: `cat-zoomies/film/hud.mjs#energyBar` + `levelColor` (the same code is in `mosquito-buzz/film/hud.mjs`). The `ping/film/hud.mjs#bars` alt was not needed.
- Lib: `lib/zoomies-meter.mjs` (new). It reuses `otext`/`tw` from `lib/zoomies-hud.mjs` unchanged. Changes: painted into an offscreen card (mask) and blitted at size z, like `lib/catname-chart.mjs`. The warm overfull halo is a separate screen-space pass that mixes the picture under it. The film words ENERGY and MAX are removed; the label comes only from s.label. The overfull look is driven by a continuous `ov`, not by level > 1. The red frame pulse is max(the film's automatic low-level pulse, warn).
- States: show (half, yellow-orange) · fill (to 92 %, green, rise) · drain (to 5 %, red, dim) · full (100 %, glow halo + shimmer, ding) · warn (red sliver, fast red frame blink, blip). The fill grows in from empty while the card enters.
- Check: `jumps 0 pops 0 pure true footprint ok` (declared 94×28, measured 96×30), about 23 ms/frame on the sheet.
- Visible issues: it is a thin card (about 190×50 px at size m), so it reads as a game HUD line rather than a big infographic. Growing in from empty passes through red for a few frames. Long labels (3 words) overhang the bar and widen the card.
- Shared change I would propose: `value: false` on purpose. For `value: true`, contract.mjs (lines 166 and 236) makes a spoken number mandatory and drops every non-show key without one. That would freeze a meter with no number at «show». The number path is implemented but dormant: when s.value is set, it is drawn right-aligned above the bar end and dissolves in with fill, and unit % sets the fill level. I checked it in a scratch render (20%, 80%, 3 H). An item flag such as `valueOptional` would enable it.

## motion-path (prop) · agent B2b
- Source: `animals-weight/film/levels/meadow-kit.mjs#dotted` (light 2-px dots with a 1-px dark outline along a polyline, drawn up to a fraction). From `mario-jump/film/render.mjs#drawTraces` I took only the generic 2-px trace-dot look, nothing of the game. Inlined in `items/motion-path.mjs`, no lib file.
- Changes: in the film, each dot popped in at full strength. Here every dot dissolves in over about 2.5 dot spacings behind the moving head, and out from the tail on «fade». A fixed 28 dots are spread evenly, so when the arc rises or curls into a loop the dots slide instead of appearing or vanishing. The curve is a throw-like parabola that always bulges up the screen. A loop-the-loop is blended in on the chord's upper side. A yellow three-dot chevron rides the head. Dot size follows the zoom; it crossfades only in a narrow band between pixel sizes. The chord aims at s.look, eased by lookK; with no target it points right and slightly up.
- States: draw (initial: low arc, draws itself dot by dot over about 1.2 s after entering) · arc (high throw arc, swish) · loop (curls into a loop, whoosh) · march (the dots flow toward the target, dissolving in at the tail and out at the end, tick) · fade (erased dot by dot from the tail, then the chevron, dim).
- Check: `jumps 0 pops 0 pure true footprint ok` (declared 82×64, measured 69×94 for the sheet's diagonal aim), about 20 ms/frame on the sheet.
- Visible issues: a steep aim gives a short, almost straight line (straight up for a target right above), and the loop then flattens to a wiggle. The footprint changes with the aim: a level aim is about 90 px half-width. At size m the dots are small (2 px); they read as a trajectory but not from far away. The sheet's close-up tile is empty because fade is the last state (I checked the zoom separately at z 1.3 and 1.85: the dots stay crisp).

## scent-trail (prop, effect) · agent B2e
- Source: `dogs/wet-nose/film/fx.mjs#particles` (+ `bit`, `SMELL_COLS`, `rnd`). The film only had loose 3-px smell bits; the
  wavy ribbons are new, drawn in the film's smell colours (pink, lilac, peach) with a dark outline and light core.
- Lib: `lib/wetnose-scent.mjs` (copy: import path, `bitCols` = the film bit's three tones), `lib/flow-kit.mjs` (new, shared
  with air-flow: flow frame, variable-alpha ribbon stroke, `bitL` = round bit with a zoom-continuous radius and a dither
  anchored to the bit, so a moving bit dissolves in place instead of twinkling).
- States: faint (initial; two thin wisps, ~8 bits) · strong (three thick ribbons, 18 bits, swell) · puff (ring of bits bursts
  out of the source, pop) · fade (dissolves, dim) · toward (trail bends onto the target, bits slow and gather at its end,
  whoosh). The flow phase is integrated. Bits are born and die by dissolving (life envelope). The ribbon radius follows
  travelling puffs, so widening never steps a whole ribbon by 1 px at once (this was the one jump in the first run).
- Check: `jumps 0 pops 0 pure true footprint ok (declared 70×86, measured 91×92)`. Real draw 0.5 ms at z1, 1.8 ms at z2.4.
- Visible quality: reads as cartoon smell lines with sparkly bits. Faint ribbons are half-dithered (see-through on purpose).
  Toward reaches at most ≈ 125 px past the slot: it does not touch a far target. Place it between the food and the nose.
  Bits are 3 px at the wide shot, as in the film.

## myth-stamp (prop, HUD, text) · agent B2a
- Source: `mosquito-buzz/film/name-hud.mjs#mark` (✓/✗ drawn stroke by stroke, dark outline stroke under the colour) + `ping/film/hud.mjs#stamp` (plate with a dark outline, thick coloured border, dark inside, the word in the border colour, landing from a larger scale).

- Check: `jumps 0 pops 0 pure true footprint ok (declared 90×32, measured 104×42)`. About 21 ms/frame in the sheet, harness included. An earlier version had 2 jumps at impact because the dust appeared in about 1 frame; the dust now ramps in over 3 frames.
