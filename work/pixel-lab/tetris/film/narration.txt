No human had ever beaten Tetris, until a 13-year-old broke it. The old Nintendo version from 1989 has no ending. Blocks just fall faster, until you lose. At level 29, they drop one row every frame. Holding the button moves a block only ten times a second, too slow to reach the sides. Players called it the kill screen. Then came hypertapping: shaking a finger on the button more than ten times a second. And in 2020, rolling: fingers drum the back of the controller up into the thumb, twenty times a second or more. Players went far past 29, and the game began to break. After level 138, the colors go wrong, and some levels are almost invisible. Inside, the game adds up your points with a slow loop. Past about level 155, that loop can take longer than one frame. The game loses its place in its own code, and freezes. An AI found the way first.
