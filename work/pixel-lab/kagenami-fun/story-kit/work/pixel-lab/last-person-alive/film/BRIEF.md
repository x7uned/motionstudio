## Style («Пиксель-фильм»)
- 540×960 grid, ×2 nearest → 1080×1920, 30 fps. Flat tone bands with Bayer dithering, dark 1-px outlines on objects, readable at phone size. Fine "32-bit" pixels, not chunky 8-bit blocks. **Full scenes**: the picture runs to every edge, no empty flat backgrounds, depth layers, foreground detail at the bottom edge.
- Horror mood but beautiful: dusk → night; cool moonlight (teal/indigo) vs warm firelight (orange); fog; silhouettes. Pick colours freely, but keep them coherent and readable.
- Every appearance / disappearance / state change is continuous: dithered dissolve (`fade(c, a)` from raster.mjs), eased motion. **Never a one-frame change.**
- Pure functions of t: no `Math.random`, no wall clock; use `rnd(n)` from layout.mjs and `EV` values (small offsets around words are fine).
- Illustrations, not real places or products: no logos, no brand names, no real people. No gore (it is a light scary story).
- No text in the picture except what the brief names (the scratched "300" and tally marks on the wall). No captions (the harness draws them).

## Camera convention (3rd-person shots)
`cam = { cx, cy, z, sx, sy }`. `layer(cam, p)` → `{ z, X(wx), Y(wy), wx(sx), wy(sy) }` for depth p (1 = the actors' plane where the people stand on wy = 0; p < 1 farther, parallax; p > 1 foreground). The screen point of world (wx, wy) on the actors' plane is `(sx + (wx − cx)·z, sy + (wy − cy)·z)`. Use the real cameras from `camera.mjs` for your stills.
