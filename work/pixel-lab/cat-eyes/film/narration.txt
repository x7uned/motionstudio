Your cat's eyes don't glow in the dark. There's a mirror inside them. Behind the retina, the part of the eye that catches light, a cat has a shiny layer called the tapetum. Light passes through the retina once. Whatever it misses hits the mirror and bounces back for a second try. Some of it comes all the way out again. That's the glow you see: light from your lamp or phone, bouncing back. The pupils help too. In the dark, they open into big circles. In the sun, they close into thin slits. From slit to circle, that's about a hundred and thirty-five times more light. Your pupils manage only about fifteen. With the mirror and the pupils, a cat can see in light about six times dimmer than you need. But there's a price. Cats see less detail. To see something as sharply as you, a cat has to get about seven times closer. You don't have this mirror. That's why your eyes turn red in flash photos.
