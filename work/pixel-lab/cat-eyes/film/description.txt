Your cat's eyes don't glow in the dark. There's a mirror inside them.

Behind the retina, cats have a shiny layer called the tapetum. Light that the retina misses bounces back for a second try, and some of it comes out again: that's the glow. Their pupils go from thin slits to big circles, about 135 times more light (yours: about 15). Together, a cat can see in light about six times dimmer than you. The price: less detail.
