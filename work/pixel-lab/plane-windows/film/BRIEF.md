## The film
51.5 s vertical pixel film, one narration take (`narration.txt`, word timings `voice/words.json` — use `EV`):
> Airplane windows are round because square ones once tore planes apart. In 1954, two Comets, the world's first jet airliners, broke apart in the sky. On every flight, the cabin is pumped up with air, like a balloon, and let down again when you land. Each time, the walls stretch a tiny bit. Sharp corners collect that stress in one spot. Flight after flight, tiny cracks grew from the sharp corner of a window, until the metal tore open. Round corners spread the stress evenly, so today every airliner has rounded windows. And that tiny hole? Your window has three layers. The hole in the middle one lets air through, so the outer layer carries all the pressure, and the middle one waits as a backup. It also lets moisture out, so your window doesn't fog up. That little hole isn't a flaw. It's a safety feature. Liked this video? Send it to a friend, and follow so you don't miss the next one.

Tone: curious, clear, a little dramatic in the middle (the crack), reassuring at the end. Bright day at cruise altitude (sky tod 2.4 + the sun disc at screen (440, 120) are drawn by the harness). **Facts are illustrated, not measured:** no real airline livery or logo, not to scale; the 1954 crashes are shown with respect (no fire, no people, no debris rain — two pieces drift apart and fade).
