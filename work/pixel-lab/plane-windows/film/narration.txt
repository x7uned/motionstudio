Airplane windows are round because square ones once tore planes apart. In 1954, two Comets, the world's first jet airliners, broke apart in the sky. On every flight, the cabin is pumped up with air, like a balloon, and let down again when you land. Each time, the walls stretch a tiny bit. Sharp corners collect that stress in one spot. Flight after flight, tiny cracks grew from the sharp corner of a window, until the metal tore open. Round corners spread the stress evenly, so today every airliner has rounded windows. And that tiny hole? Your window has three layers. The hole in the middle one lets air through, so the outer layer carries all the pressure, and the middle one waits as a backup.
