Buy your cat a fancy new bed, and it sits in the box it came in. That's not random. When a cat feels stressed, it doesn't fight. It hides. In a shelter in the Netherlands, scientists gave some new cats a box to hide in. Those cats calmed down in about three days. The cats without a box took about two weeks. A box is also warm. Cats feel best at around thirty to thirty-eight degrees Celsius, much warmer than your living room. Curled up in a small box, a cat keeps its own heat close. And for a hunter, a box is the perfect hiding spot: from inside, the cat sees everything, and nobody sees the cat. The strangest part? Scientists asked owners to put paper shapes on the floor. Cats sat inside a drawn square. Some even sat in a square that wasn't really there, just four corners that trick the eye. So keep the box. Trust me:
