Your cat knows its name. It just ignores you. In twenty nineteen, scientists in Tokyo tested seventy-seven cats. A hidden speaker played four normal words, each with the same rhythm as the cat's name. With every word, the ears moved a little less. The cat got bored. Then the speaker said its name, and the ears and the head turned again. So cats can tell their name from other words. It even worked with a stranger's voice. But look at what the cats did. Most moved only their ears and head. Few meowed or moved their tail, and most didn't take a single step. Years earlier, the same team played cats three strangers calling their name, then their owner. The cats perked up for their owner. But they still just turned their heads. The scientists think cats, unlike dogs, were never bred to obey us. They answer when they want to.
