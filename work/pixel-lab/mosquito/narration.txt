If mosquitoes always bite you first, it's not bad luck. In one lab test, they liked one person over a hundred times more than the people they liked least. Only females bite. They need the protein in blood to make eggs. And they choose you before they ever taste you. First, your breath. Each time you breathe out, you leave a trail of carbon dioxide, and she follows it. That gas switches on her eyes. Now she flies toward red and orange, the colors your skin reflects, and ignores green, blue and purple. Up close, she feels your body heat. But the final choice is your smell. Scientists in New York collected the smell of sixty-four people on nylon sleeves. The favorites had much more of three fatty acids on their skin. And three years later, the same people were still the favorites. So she isn't picking your blood. She's picking your skin. Send this to the friend who always gets bitten first.
