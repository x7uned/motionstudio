In Super Mario Bros., holding jump makes gravity weaker. But only on the way up. Every standing jump starts at the same speed. The button doesn't push Mario higher. It changes how fast gravity slows him down. While you hold it, gravity stays weak, so Mario keeps rising, about four blocks high. Let go early, and gravity gets more than three times stronger right away. Mario stops rising sooner. A quick tap lifts him less than two blocks. That's how one button gives you a tiny hop or a big jump. At the top, the strong gravity comes back, even if you keep holding. Mario goes up for half a second, but comes down in just a third. Running changes the jump too. The game checks your speed the moment you leave the ground. With a running start, Mario launches faster and jumps about five blocks high.
