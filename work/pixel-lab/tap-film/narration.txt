You tap your card maybe ten times a day. Ever wondered what happens between the tap and "approved"? Let's follow one tap. You're in Tokyo, buying a coffee with a card from your bank in London. The chip signs this purchase with a one-time code, and the terminal sends it to the shop's bank. That bank doesn't know you, so it passes the request to the card network, like Visa or Mastercard: a web of data centers linked by cables on the ocean floor. The network sends it across the planet to your bank in London, where it's the middle of the night. Your bank checks the code, your balance, and whether this looks like you. Then "approved" races back the same way. Beep. More than twenty thousand kilometers, in a few seconds. But no money has moved yet. Your bank only put a hold on it. The real money follows a day or two later, when the banks settle up, and the shop pays a small fee along the way. So every tap is a trip around the world first, and money second.
