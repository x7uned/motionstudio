## Факты и источники
- Старые казино Стрипа без окон и часов. Bill Friedman (Designing Casinos to Dominate the Competition, 2000) называет «специально, чтобы ты потерял время» мифом: этого требовали серьёзные игроки. https://www.casino.org/news/vegas-myths-busted-casinos-sunlight-and-clocks-to-trick-gamblers/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Friedman
- Кислород: миф из романа Пьюзо «Fools Die» (1978); замеры в Caesars Palace, Flamingo, Bellagio, Cosmopolitan — 20,9%, как снаружи. https://www.casino.org/news/busting-vegas-myths-casinos-pump-in-extra-oxygen
- Принципы Friedman: лабиринт лучше прямых проходов, низкие потолки, игры сразу у входа. https://gizmodo.com/how-casinos-use-design-psychology-to-get-you-to-gambling-1667182023
- Путь к ресепшену/лифтам часто через зал (планы Paris, Venetian, Harrah's) — «often», не «always».
- Фирменные запахи: Las Vegas Sun 2010 (Bellagio, Mirage, Mandalay Bay). https://lasvegassun.com/news/2010/jan/06/if-it-smells-northern-italy-it-must-be-bellagio/
- Музыка: Dixon, Trigg & Griffiths 2007, Int. Gambling Studies 7(3):315–326 — быстрый темп ускорял ставки (размер ставки не менялся). https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/intgms/v7y2007i3p315-326.html
- «Playground»-дизайн (Roger Thomas: Mirage, Bellagio, Wynn): высокие потолки, свет, часы. Исследования Университета Гуэлфа (Finlay и др.): в «playground»-залах у добровольцев сильнее намерение остаться и играть, восстанавливающие сцены (деревья, вода, небо) делали людей спокойнее и склоннее задержаться. https://news.uoguelph.ca/2012/07/casinos-atmosphere-affects-gamblers-behaviour

**Оговорки:** «can make people want to stay» — это оценки и намерения добровольцев, не выручка казино, поэтому «can» и «want». Скорость музыки — маленький лабораторный опыт, в 2016 не повторился; говорим «in one test». Миф про ковёр и «+45% от запаха» не используем.

# Film 3 — How the casino building keeps you inside (research, checked 2026-10-27)

Method: web search + page fetches. Not read directly: Friedman's book and the Hirsch paper (only press/secondary).
Verdicts: TRUE / PARTLY / MYTH / DEBATED. Mostly US (Las Vegas) unless noted.

## Claims

1. **No windows, no clocks (old Strip casinos)** — PARTLY. Old Strip casinos (1950s–80s) were dark with no windows or clocks. But the claim "built to make you lose track of time" is disputed: Friedman himself calls it a myth and says serious players demanded it. Bellagio-era casinos have skylights and clocks, so "all casinos" is false.
   - https://www.casino.org/news/vegas-myths-busted-casinos-sunlight-and-clocks-to-trick-gamblers/
   - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Friedman
   - https://www.mentalfloss.com/why-casinos-dont-have-windows

2. **Friedman's maze + low ceilings + games at the entrance** — TRUE (as a published design doctrine). *Designing Casinos to Dominate the Competition* (2000): "maze layout beats long straight aisles", "low ceilings beat high ceilings", gambling equipment immediately inside the entrance, games as the decor. Idea: curiosity about the next corner, small walled-off areas that hold attention. (Friedman built this on observation of what made money in Reno/Vegas, not a lab trial.) "Exits hard to see" is a popular summary, not a quoted principle; I did not find it in the sources. Mark as folk.
   - https://gizmodo.com/how-casinos-use-design-psychology-to-get-you-to-gambling-1667182023
   - https://www.casino.org/news/vegas-myths-busted-casinos-sunlight-and-clocks-to-trick-gamblers/

3. **"Playground" design (Wynn/Roger Thomas) is the opposite** — TRUE. From 1980 (Golden Nugget), then Mirage, Bellagio, Wynn: high ceilings, daylight in some areas, clear sight lines, antique clocks. Thomas argued maze casinos made people feel trapped.
   - https://gizmodo.com/how-casinos-use-design-psychology-to-get-you-to-gambling-1667182023
   - https://www.casino.org/news/vegas-myths-busted-casinos-sunlight-and-clocks-to-trick-gamblers/

4. **Finlay / Marmurek / Kanetkar / Londerville research (Univ. of Guelph)** — PARTLY, careful wording. They compared "playground" vs "gaming" (low ceilings, crowded) designs. Findings: (a) 2007 *Int. Gambling Studies* — music RAISED rated at-risk gambling intentions in the playground design but LOWERED them in the gaming design; (b) 2009 follow-up (lighting, colour, crowding, symmetry) — playground casinos were always more likely to encourage gambling intentions, whatever small tweaks; (c) restorative scenes (trees, water, sky) made people feel calmer and more likely to linger and return. These are ratings and intentions of volunteers (videos, one field study of ~180 slot players), NOT measured casino revenue. No windows result found.
   - https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/intgms/v7y2007i1p113-122.html
   - https://news.uoguelph.ca/2012/07/casinos-atmosphere-affects-gamblers-behaviour
   - Note: so "bright and airy" is not safe for the player — the research suggests it may keep people longer.

5. **Busy, ugly carpets** — MYTH (as intent); folk explanation. "Eyes up on the machines" / "no sitting on the floor" are theories with no operator or study support found. Casino.org's Vital Vegas author says no manager he knows designs carpet for that. Practical reason: patterns hide stains and wear (many spilled drinks), and carpet can be a brand look. Hide-stains = plausible, not formally proven.
   - https://www.casino.org/news/vegas-myths-busted-casino-carpets-are-designed-to-trick-you-into-gambling-more/ (page returned 404 for me later; search snippet used)
   - https://www.lasvegasadvisor.com/question/2005-07-31/

6. **Fast music -> faster betting** — PARTLY. Dixon, Trigg & Griffiths (2007), *International Gambling Studies* 7(3):315–326: 60 people, virtual roulette; faster tempo -> faster bets; bet size and total spending NOT affected. Spenwyn, Barrett & Griffiths (2010), *Int. J. Mental Health & Addiction* 8:107–118: 56 people, online roulette; tempo sped up bets; fast music + red light together gave the fastest betting; amount per spin unchanged. Not replicated: Bramley, Dibben & Rowe (2016), *Psychology of Music*, 144 people: tempo did not change speed, spend or risk. Small lab studies, not real casinos.
   - https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/intgms/v7y2007i3p315-326.html
   - https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007%2Fs11469-009-9226-0
   - https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0305735616632897

7. **Hirsch scent study, "+45% money"** — DEBATED / unreplicated. Alan Hirsch, "Effects of ambient odors on slot-machine usage in a Las Vegas casino", *Psychology & Marketing* 1995. Las Vegas Hilton, one weekend (tested Oct 1991): in the area with a pleasant scent, slot money was ~45% higher than on the weekends before and after; second scent and an unscented area showed no change. Problems: one casino, one weekend vs other weekends, not randomised; the scent maker (AromaSys) said "no aromas make people gamble"; Hirsch won't name the scent; no replication in 30 years; a 2017 lottery-task study with citrus scent found no effect on risk choice.
   - https://www.casino.org/news/myths-we-couldnt-bust-in-2025/
   - https://www.upi.com/Archives/1992/09/07/Researchers-find-scent-of-money/6750715838400/
   - https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/75293/1/MPRA_paper_75293.pdf

8. **Casinos do use signature scents** — TRUE (practice). Las Vegas Sun (2010): devices on ventilation systems at nearly every Strip resort spread scent; Mirage smells "Polynesian", Mandalay Bay "Southeast Asian", Bellagio "Northern Italy" (AromaSys supplier). Proof they use scent is solid; proof scent makes you gamble more is NOT.
   - https://lasvegassun.com/news/2010/jan/06/if-it-smells-northern-italy-it-must-be-bellagio/ (fetch paywalled; from search summary)
   - https://www.unlv.edu/news/article/scents-and-sensibility

9. **Red vs blue light** — PARTLY / old and small. Stark, Saunders & Wookey (1982), *Current Psychological Research* 2:95–99: 28 volunteers, card game; under red light they bet more money, more often, and picked riskier odds than under blue. Spenwyn 2010: red light only mattered together with fast music. A 2025 Flinders study (15 people) points the other way for blue-enriched light. Not proven for casinos.
   - https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF03186749
   - https://www.casino.org/vitalvegas/study-casinos-use-blue-heavy-light-to-bamboozle-gamblers/

10. **Hotel check-in, restaurants, toilets so you walk past games** — PARTLY / common pattern, no study. Floor plans of many Strip resorts (Paris, Venetian/Palazzo, Harrah's) put check-in and elevators next to or through the casino; Friedman's doctrine puts games right inside the entrance. Not universal (Luxor atrium elevators; Mohegan plan puts toilets at entrance vestibules). Treat as "often", not "always".
    - https://www.nationalboard.org/general-meeting/assets/img/Harrahs_floor_plans.pdf
    - https://gizmodo.com/how-casinos-use-design-psychology-to-get-you-to-gambling-1667182023

11. **"Casinos pump extra oxygen"** — MYTH. Origin: Mario Puzo's 1978 novel *Fools Die*. Gambling.com measured 20.9% oxygen (same as outside) in Caesars Palace, Flamingo, Bellagio, Cosmopolitan. Raising oxygen 1% in a casino ~ 40,000 m3 gas/day; more oxygen makes fires burn hotter (insurance/fire risk). UNLV gaming-law expert Tony Cabot: "not true".
    - https://www.casino.org/news/busting-vegas-myths-casinos-pump-in-extra-oxygen
    - https://www.gambling.com/news/gambling-com-sets-the-record-straight-on-las-vegas-casino-myths

## Extra facts (checked)
- **Slots replaced tables as the money floor**; Schüll's *Addiction by Design* (Princeton UP, 2012, 15 yrs of fieldwork) describes the "machine zone" and an industry that tunes machines, chairs, layout and "ambience" to keep people playing without interruption. TRUE (book-length ethnography). https://muse.jhu.edu/book/61897
- **Losses disguised as wins (LDW)**: on multi-line slots you bet 20 lines, win back less than you bet, and the machine still plays a win jingle. Dixon et al. 2010 *Addiction*: body (skin-conductance) reacts to LDWs like real wins; Dixon et al. 2013 *J Gambling Studies*: with sound, people overestimated how often they won (24% vs 15% silent). TRUE. https://uwaterloo.ca/news/news/music-gamblers-ears
- **Dealer clothes have no pockets / small apron**: anti-theft, so chips can't be pocketed. TRUE (dealer accounts, NPR). https://www.npr.org/transcripts/246809717 (belongs to tables)
- **Wynn's chips contain RFID (2005)**: casino can follow chips and spot fakes. TRUE. https://www.tampabay.com/archive/2005/06/20/wired-chips-track-high-rollers/
- **Playground rooms use real clocks**: Thomas installed antique timepieces at Bellagio. TRUE per Gizmodo. (Opposite of the "no clocks" myth.)
- Not verified (no solid source found): "cashier cage placed deep inside", "ATMs on the floor are placed by design", "slots at ends of rows" as a measured effect. Do not state as fact.

## Myths to avoid
- "Casinos remove clocks/windows to trick your body clock" — the designer who wrote the book says no.
- "Pumped-in oxygen" — false.
- "Carpets are designed to keep your eyes up" — no evidence.
- "Scent makes you spend 45% more" — one unreplicated study.
- "Music makes you bet more money" — it may speed betting in small lab tests, bet size did not change.
- "Red light makes you gamble more" — one 1982 study of 28 people.

## Best 6 facts for a simple 55-second film
1. Old Las Vegas casinos had no windows and no clocks, but the man who wrote the design book says players asked for that (Friedman; casino.org).
2. His rule: no straight aisles, a maze, low ceilings, and games right inside the door (Friedman 2000).
3. Wynn's team did the opposite from 1980 — high ceilings, daylight, clear paths, clocks — and a University of Guelph team found calm, "playground" rooms made volunteers more likely to want to stay and gamble.
4. In a small lab test, faster music made people place roulette bets faster, but they did not bet more money (Dixon 2007).
5. Casinos really do pipe in scents — the Bellagio smells of "Northern Italy" — but the famous "+45%" scent result comes from one weekend in one casino and was never repeated.
6. Casinos do NOT pump in extra oxygen — measurements in four big Vegas casinos showed 20.9%, same as outside air.
