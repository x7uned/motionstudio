A bee's buzz is just its wings, beating two hundred and thirty times a second. Every flap pushes air, and that makes a sound. Let's line up wings from slow to fast, and listen. An albatross mostly glides. When it flaps, it's about two and a half times a second. You could count every beat. A pigeon flaps about six times a second. A butterfly, about ten. A damselfly, about twenty. And around twenty a second, something changes: the beats blur into one sound. A hummingbird beats its wings about forty-four times a second. That low hum gave the bird its name. A ladybug: about seventy-five. A bumblebee: about a hundred and fifty, a deep buzz. A honeybee: two hundred and thirty. A mosquito: about five hundred. That's the whine in your ear, and on a warm night it gets even higher. And a tiny midge beats its wings about a thousand times a second. Faster wings, higher note. Every animal is a little different, so these numbers are rough. And that bee in your garden?
