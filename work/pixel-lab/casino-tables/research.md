# How casinos beat you at the tables and in your head: fact check (2026-10-27)

Method: web search plus page fetches. Some PDFs/pages could not be opened (marked "not read directly"). Nothing below is invented; where a claim has no good source it is marked DEBATED or MYTH.

## 1. Claims with verdicts

**Roulette "last numbers" boards feed the gambler's fallacy** - DEBATED (effect on bettors TRUE, "casinos show them for this reason" UNPROVEN)
- Plain: Each spin is independent, so after five blacks black is still 18/38 on an American wheel. Real casino video shows people DO bet against streaks, but I found no controlled study of the boards, and no casino statement on why they show them. Claims that the board exists "to trick you" come from popular gambling sites only.
- Croson and Sundali (2005), "The Gambler's Fallacy and the Hot Hand: Empirical Data from Casinos", J. Risk and Uncertainty 30(3), 195-209. Roulette players on casino video bet more against a colour after a long streak: https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/jrisku/v30y2005i3p195-209.html
- Duke probability page (5 blacks, still 18/38): https://sites.math.duke.edu/education/postcalc/probability/fallacy.html
- Safe wording: "The board shows past spins. Past spins change nothing."

**Monte Carlo 1913, 26 blacks** - TRUE (as the standard story)
- Plain: On 18 Aug 1913 the ball fell on black 26 times in a row; gamblers lost "millions of francs" betting on red. Wikipedia's source is a BBC article; no contemporary primary quote. Odds of the run about 1 in 68 million (single-zero wheel). Keep wording "reportedly".
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambler%27s_fallacy

**Blackjack: house edge comes mainly from the player acting first** - TRUE
- Plain: You play first. If you bust you lose at once, even if the dealer would have busted too. Double bust is not a tie. Wizard of Odds FAQ: "The dealer has the advantage because the player has to go first... If both of you bust it isn't a tie, but YOU lose."
- https://wizardofodds.com/ask-the-wizard/blackjack/general/
- Note: it is the biggest single reason, but basic-strategy edge is small (about 0.4-0.5% on typical rules; Atlantic City 0.43%).

**6:5 vs 3:2 blackjack payout** - TRUE
- Plain: A natural 21 on a $10 bet pays $15 at 3:2 but only $12 at 6:5. Wizard of Odds: 6:5 raises the house edge by 1.39 points (example 0.05% to 1.44%), "by far the worst blackjack game in Vegas". Basic strategy does not fix it.
- https://wizardofodds.com/ask-the-wizard/blackjack/variants/

**Card counting is legal but casinos can ban counters; shufflers defeat counting** - TRUE (US)
- Plain: Counting in your head is not a crime in the US (a device is). Casinos are private property and can "back off" (no blackjack) or "bar" (leave the building) you. They cannot detain you for it. Continuous shuffling machines (CSM) mean the count never builds. Exception: New Jersey (Uston v. Resorts International, 1982) limits barring; casinos use extra decks and early shuffles there.
- Las Vegas Advisor, "Is card counting illegal?": https://www.lasvegasadvisor.com/faq-gambling-counting-illegal/
- CSM: Arnold Snyder in Las Vegas Advisor: counting is useless vs a CSM: https://www.lasvegasadvisor.com/question/house-advantage-shuffling-machine/ (the Wizard's CSM figure, about 0.014%, is quoted second-hand there; I did not read the Wizard page directly).
- Ken Uston / NJ ruling summary: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Uston

**Craps: shake harder for high numbers, softer for low (Henslin 1967)** - TRUE, with a caveat
- Citation: Henslin, J. M. (1967). Craps and magic. American Journal of Sociology 73(3), 316-330. Observational study of St. Louis cab drivers who played craps. (Reference format confirmed via Clark and Wohl 2021, PMC9292938; I could not open the old PDF text to read the passage myself.)
- Modern test: Hayes, Walasek, Browne and Newall (2025), "The active foundations of the illusion of control: an experimental test of the Henslin effect", Addiction Research and Theory 33(3), 223-230. 1,692 people shook a phone as dice; they shook about 4.1% harder when aiming for six than for one. https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/188285
- Plain: Throwing harder cannot change a fair die. Effect is real but small (4%).

**Langer 1975 "illusion of control"** - TRUE
- Citation: Langer, E. J. (1975). The illusion of control. J. Personality and Social Psychology 32(2), 311-328.
- Study 2: office workers with a $1 lottery ticket. People who CHOSE their ticket asked $8.67 on average to sell it back; people who were GIVEN one asked $1.96. Later critique (Klusowski, Small and Simmons 2021) says choice alone may not cause it, so say "often", not "always".
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9292938/

**Dealers clap hands / show palms ("clearing hands")** - TRUE
- Plain: Rule, not just habit. Ohio code 3772-11-05 makes dealers clear their hands, fingers spread, both sides shown, in view of surveillance, when entering or leaving a game. Kansas has a similar rule. In a 2025 Pennsylvania case a dealer was charged after showing only one open hand (news report). https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-3772-11-05 (page timed out when fetched; text taken from search result - treat as "not read directly"). Kansas: https://krgc.kansas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/20120213_112-108-39.pdf
- Dealer uniforms without pockets: NOT FOUND in any source. Skip it.

**"Eye in the sky"** - TRUE
- Plain: Overhead cameras watch tables, staff and players to catch cheating and theft. Nevada Regulation 5 requires tables to be monitored and recorded clearly enough to identify dealers and patrons and read wagers, card values and outcomes (summary from search result; PDF not readable by my tool). Phrase "eye in the sky" is industry slang, not legal wording.
- https://www.gaming.nv.gov/siteassets/content/regs/regulation-5---surveillance-standards-as-of-04-26.pdf
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casino_security

**Near miss** - TRUE
- Clark et al. (2009), Neuron 61, "Gambling near-misses enhance motivation to gamble and recruit win-related brain circuitry". 15 people on a slot task: near-misses felt worse but raised the wish to play, and lit up win-related brain areas (ventral striatum, insula); only when the player had a choice. Small sample, slot machine task (not table games). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2658737/

**Rituals (blowing on dice) / hot hand** - PARTLY
- Documented: Henslin describes magical practices by craps players. Croson and Sundali found both hot-hand and gambler's fallacy bets in roulette video. I found NO source that casinos "encourage" rituals; do not say they do.

**Free play / demo mode shows better odds than real money** - MYTH in the UK, DEBATED elsewhere
- UK Gambling Commission RTS 6A: play-for-free games must use the same rules as the real-money game and "accurately represent the likelihood of winning and prize distribution". Only supplier demo sites showing above-normal returns must say they are demos. https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/standards/remote-gambling-and-software-technical-standards/rts-6-result-determination-for-play-for-free-games
- I found no regulator report proving a specific casino cheated. Affiliate sites make claims without sources. Say: "In the UK the rules say free play must match real play."

**Bonus with 35x wagering** - TRUE (explanation); UK now caps it
- Plain: $100 bonus at 35x means you must bet $3,500 before you can cash out. Each bet loses a little on average, so much of the bonus is eaten. UK Gambling Commission: wagering on bonuses capped at 10x from 19 Jan 2026 because high requirements "could confuse consumers" and make people gamble longer. https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/news/article/gambling-promotions-to-be-safer-and-simpler
- The 35x math is my arithmetic, not a regulator quote.

**Higher table minimums at busy hours** - TRUE (US Strip, anecdotal)
- WSJ (May 2023, via CDC Gaming): some Vegas casinos raised minimums during busy times and cut dealer tables; a floor supervisor said the goal is about 80% table occupancy. Players already seated are often kept at the old minimum. https://cdcgaming.com/brief/why-people-are-losing-more-to-casinos-on-the-las-vegas-strip/
- https://www.lasvegasadvisor.com/question/blackjack-tables/
- "Pay less on blackjack at busy hours": NOT sourced; the real thing is 6:5 tables vs 3:2 tables, not time of day. Skip.

## 2. Extra surprising facts

1. **Casino dice**: transparent or translucent, sharp "razor" edges, 3/4 inch (+/- 0.0002 in), each die carries a serial number and the casino logo to stop swapping. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dice . South Australia's regulator requires a serial number on each die and clear cellulose acetate: https://www.slga.com/-/media/slga/files/permits-and-licences/integrity-standards/3455-dice-game-standards-june-2022.pdf (not read directly). "Replaced often": not documented in these sources.
2. **Cards are used for a shift or a day**, then cancelled with a punched hole or clipped corner so they cannot come back, and often sold in gift shops for about $1-5. Practice varies by casino; no regulation found. https://www.lasvegasadvisor.com/question/used-cards/
3. **Joseph Jagger and the biased wheel**: a Yorkshire textile man who logged the numbers, found a wheel that favoured some, and reportedly won over 2 million francs at Monte Carlo. Date: Wikipedia says about 1880-81 (NOT 1873; some sources say 1873). The wheel-bias method is "thought", the winnings "reputed". The casino later had makers fit movable frets. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Jagger
4. **MIT blackjack team**: began 1979-80; roles of spotter (counts), controller, and big player (bets big when the deck is rich). Casinos barred players, and investigators matched faces using MIT yearbooks. Many numbers on that page are "citation needed". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Blackjack_Team
5. **Baccarat and Macau**: baccarat made about 91% of Macau casino income in 2014 (Wikipedia); VIP baccarat share was 66% of gross gaming revenue in 2013 and fell as junkets shrank (asgam). Banker bet edge about 1.06% after the 5% commission. No source gives a clear "why" (cultural preference is a guess). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baccarat_(card_game) , https://asgam.com/2024/07/17/further-decline-in-vip-baccarat-behind-2q24-slowdown-in-macau-gaming-revenues/
6. **Dealer rotation**: dealers move tables often to make dealer-player collusion hard; poker "downs" are usually 30 min, sometimes 20 or 60. A fixed 20-60 min rule for all games is NOT documented. https://poker.fandom.com/wiki/Down
7. **6:5 blackjack tables cost you 1.39 points more** than 3:2 tables (see above); the same game, a much worse deal.
8. Dealer "tip economy" and "why baccarat dominates": not researched to a reliable source; do not use.

## Myths to avoid
- "A colour is due" / "the board helps you win".
- "Casinos built the number board specifically to trick you": no proof, say "bettors react to it".
- "Dealers have no pockets": no source.
- "Casinos make card counting illegal": it is not illegal; they just ban counters.
- "Online free play is rigged to look better": UK rules say it must match; no proof for a given site.
- "Casinos encourage dice blowing": undocumented.
- "Joseph Jagger, 1873": dates differ; use "1880s" or just "in the 1800s".
- "Pay less on blackjack at busy hours": unsourced.

## Best 6 facts for a simple 55-second film
1. In blackjack you play first, so if you bust you lose even when the dealer busts later.
2. A 6:5 blackjack table pays $12 on a $10 bet instead of $15, which adds about 1.4 points to the house edge.
3. After five blacks in a row, the next spin is still almost 50-50 (18 in 38); the wheel has no memory, and in 1913 Monte Carlo players reportedly lost millions betting red after 26 blacks.
4. Craps players shake dice harder for big numbers (about 4% harder in a 2025 test of 1,692 people), but the throw cannot change a fair die.
5. People who pick their own lottery ticket want about four times more to sell it than people who are handed one (Langer 1975: $8.67 vs $1.96).
6. Dealers show open palms to the cameras above, and Ohio's rule requires it, so nobody can say a chip went into a pocket.

Spare: Casino dice are clear, razor-sharp and have serial numbers; a 35x bonus means betting $3,500 to cash out $100.
