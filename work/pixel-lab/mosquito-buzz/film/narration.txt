That mosquito whine in your ear is a love song. It's just not for you. Every time you breathe out, you send out a cloud of carbon dioxide. A female mosquito follows it to your face. At night, your face is often the only part not under the blanket. Up close, she can feel your warmth. Now listen. The whine isn't her voice. It's her wings. They beat about five hundred times a second, and that's the note you hear. She is so quiet that you only hear her right by your ear. And she circles your head, so the sound comes and goes. That note is meant for males. A male hears it with his fluffy antennae. When they meet, both change their notes until the notes fit together. So she isn't singing to you.
