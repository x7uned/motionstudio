Boop! Why is your dog's nose always cold and wet? It's not a cold. It's a tool. Inside, glands keep the nose covered in a thin layer of mucus. Smells are tiny bits floating in the air. A wet nose catches them, like sticky tape catches dust. Then your dog licks its nose, and the tongue carries those smells to a second smell organ on the roof of its mouth. A dog has up to three hundred million smell cells. You have about six million. Even breathing out helps: the air leaves through slits on the sides, so it doesn't blow the smell away. A wet nose also stays cold, colder than the air around it. In 2020, scientists found that dogs can sense faint heat from a warm object more than a meter and a half away, and that cold nose may be how they do it. So a dry nose doesn't mean a sick dog.
