Why does your dog spin in circles before it lies down? It isn't lost. It's making a bed. Long before sofas, a dog's wild relatives slept outside. Wolves still do it: they walk in circles to press tall grass or snow flat into a nest. Your dog still runs that old plan, even on a soft pillow. A scientist named Stanley Coren tested it with sixty-two dogs. On a flat, smooth mat, only about one dog in five turned around before lying down. On a lumpy, shaggy rug, more than half did, and some even dug at it first. Bumps mean more circles. Then the circle ends in a curl: nose tucked in, tail over the face. That keeps the heat in on a cold night. Some experts think circling also lets a dog check for danger, but no one has tested that yet. One warning: if your dog circles a lot and still can't settle, ask a vet. It may be in pain. Otherwise, it's just making its bed, round and round, and round…
