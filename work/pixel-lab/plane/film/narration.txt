Ever wondered what actually happens if you forget airplane mode? Spoiler: the plane won't fall out of the sky. Your phone just has a really bad flight. About ten kilometres up, it can barely hold on to a tower, so it starts shouting: it turns its radio up to full power and keeps calling out, again and again. That's why your battery melts on a long flight. Cell towers aim their antennas at the ground, and the network expects you to pass a few of them in a car, not fly over dozens at nine hundred kilometres an hour. From up here, your phone can reach many towers at once, and that confuses the network below. That's why, in the US, the first ban came from the FCC, the telecom regulator, not from aviation. Then there's the cockpit. A phone hunting for a signal sends short radio bursts, and pilots say they hear them as a buzz in their headsets, like a mosquito, right while air traffic control is talking. One phone is just an annoyance. A plane carries two hundred. Some planes now have their own tiny cell tower in the cabin, linked to a satellite, so phones can whisper instead of shout.
