# CRT research (2026-10-26, research subagent; web sources)

- Shadow mask / aperture grille, RGB triads: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_mask , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aperture_grille
- 240p: consoles drew every other line → dark gaps ("scanlines"), a symptom not a feature; faint on well-focused sets: https://www.hdretrovision.com/240p
- Blur/glow from beam spread + soft composite/RF: safe wording "the TV softened and blended neighbouring pixels".
- Sonic 1 waterfalls: vertical-stripe dithering (VDP has no partial transparency), composite blur reads as see-through water: https://rasterscroll.com/mdgraphics/graphical-effects/transparency/ , https://timeextension.com/news/2024/10/what-do-you-see-in-sonic-the-hedgehogs-waterfalls
- Developer quotes (https://crtdatabase.com/articles/art-design): Naoto Ohshima (Sonic designer) "we made use of the CRT's blurriness"; Eternal Champions "colors would blend together"; Vectorman "vertical lines blend automatically on the TV"; Streets of Rage 2 checkerboard spotlights; Ecco checkerboard; Disney Lion King/Aladdin hash patterns → gradients. Do NOT credit the waterfall to Ayano Koshiro.
- Metal Slug Oda "add 0.5 of a pixel" — secondhand (Tumblr translation), avoid or hedge.
- Counterpoint: not universal (arcade RGB monitors crisp, artists' monitors sharp). Say "many artists", never "all pixel art".
- Modern LCD: each game pixel → hard square, dither stays sharp, no gaps: https://rasterscroll.com/?p=408
- Switch Online NES/SNES CRT filters (SNES upgraded July 2025): https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2025/07/switch-onlines-snes-update-has-apparently-upgraded-the-crt-filter
- Emulator CRT shaders (RetroArch, Genesis Plus GX composite). Collectors buying CRTs, PVM/BVM prized — avoid exact prices.
- Zapper needs CRT timing (Wikipedia Light gun). "Melee players still prefer CRTs" only as hedged.
- Myths to avoid: all pixel art for CRT; scanlines intended; every CRT looked the same (RF/composite/S-Video/RGB differ).
