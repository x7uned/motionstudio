Old games really did look better on old TVs. And many artists planned it that way. An old TV didn't show sharp squares. A beam of electrons painted the picture line by line, onto tiny red, green and blue dots. So pixels next to each other blurred together. Artists used that blur. Look at the waterfalls in Sonic the Hedgehog. The console couldn't make see-through water. So the artists drew thin stripes: water, background, water, background. On a TV, the stripes melted into clear water. Sonic's designer, Naoto Ohshima, said they made use of the TV's blurriness. Other teams drew checkerboards to make new colors and soft lights. Now put the same game on a sharp modern screen. Every pixel becomes a hard square. The stripes stay stripes. That's why many new releases add a CRT filter, and why collectors buy old TVs again.
