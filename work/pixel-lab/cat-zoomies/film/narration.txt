Your cat sleeps all day, then goes crazy at three a.m. Annoying, right? But your cat isn't trying to get you. It's built to hunt. A cat sleeps about fifteen hours a day. But much of that is light sleep: its ears keep turning to every sound, ready to jump. In the wild, one mouse is a tiny meal, so a cat has to catch about ten a day. Every hunt is a long wait, then a short sprint at full power. So between hunts, the cat sleeps and saves its energy. Mice come out at dusk and at dawn, and that's when a cat's body is ready to hunt. But your cat's dinner comes from a bowl. There's no hunt, so all that saved-up energy has nowhere to go. At night, in a quiet house, it all comes out at once. Vets call it FRAPs: frenetic random activity periods. You call it the zoomies. The fix: give your cat a hunt before bed. Play with a toy on a string until it's tired, then feed it.
