Your Duck Hunt gun never fired a single shot. It only looks at the screen. Inside the barrel, a lens aims a tiny light sensor at one spot on the TV. When you pull the trigger, the whole screen goes black for one frame. In the next frame, a white box appears where the duck is. Everything else stays black. If the sensor sees that white box, you hit the duck. With two ducks, each one flashes in its own frame, so the timing tells the game which duck you hit. The whole test takes less than a tenth of a second. Why black first? To catch cheaters. Old tube TVs draw the picture one line at a time, nearly sixteen thousand lines a second, and the gun only reacts to that fast flicker. So a lamp's steady light does nothing. And if any light shows up while the screen is black, the game calls it a miss. Modern flat TVs don't flicker line by line, and they show each picture a little late.
