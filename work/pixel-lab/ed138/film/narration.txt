This new world beyond Pluto was in old photos for ten years, and nobody noticed. Its name for now is twenty twenty-six E-D one thirty-eight. On October thirteenth, an astronomer ran his search code on pictures from the Rubin Observatory, a giant telescope in Chile. The code looks at billions of tiny dots and finds the ones that belong to the same moving object. One dot was very far away, but still bright. That means it's big, maybe six hundred to fifteen hundred kilometers wide. Why such a wide guess? We don't know yet how shiny its ice is. Then the team looked back. The same dot was hiding in older pictures from another telescope, all the way back to twenty sixteen. So why did everyone miss it? It sits in front of the Milky Way, where millions of stars crowd the sky. One slow, faint dot just gets lost. Rubin photographs the same sky again and again, so anything that moves stands out. Now we need to know if it's round. If it is, it could become the sixth dwarf planet, next to Pluto, Ceres, Eris, Haumea and Makemake. Follow, so you don't miss the next new world.
