The dumbest animal on Earth has zero brain cells, and it outlived the dinosaurs. The sea sponge just sits and filters food from the water. But every step up from here unlocks one new skill. A jellyfish has no brain either, only a net of nerves. But now it can swim. The octopus has five hundred million brain cells, most of them in its arms. So it solves problems, like opening a jar. A bee's brain is the size of a sesame seed, but it can learn. It dances to show the others where the flowers are. A dog learns our words. A border collie named Chaser knew the names of one thousand and twenty-two toys. Some crows carve hooks from twigs. If you scare one, it remembers your face for years. Each dolphin has its own whistle, like a name. A friend still knows it after twenty years apart. Then a twist: an elephant has three times more brain cells than we do, but almost all of them control its body. Still, it can know itself in a mirror. A chimp can even beat us. A young chimp named Ayumu won a fast memory game against college students. And at the top, us: eighty-six billion brain cells. We plan years ahead, and we make lists like this one. But we've been here for only three hundred thousand years. And the animal at the bottom of the list?
