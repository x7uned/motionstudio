Sources:
- Sponges have no neurons; sponge-like fossils ~600 million years old: Smithsonian Magazine; Cobb, "Why sponges are animals".
- Honeybee brain ~1 million neurons, ~1 mm³; waggle dance: Menzel, Nature Reviews Neuroscience 2012 (doi 10.1038/nrn3357); von Frisch, Nobel Prize 1973.
- Octopus ~500 million neurons, about two thirds in the arms: OIST; ScienceAlert.
- Chaser the border collie knew the names of 1,022 toys: Pilley & Reid, Behavioural Processes 2011.
- New Caledonian crows make hooked stick tools: St Andrews / Edinburgh, Nature Ecology & Evolution 2018. Crows remember a threatening face for at least five years: Marzluff et al., University of Washington.
- Dolphin signature whistles used like names: King & Janik, PNAS 2013; recognised after 20+ years apart: Bruck, Proc. R. Soc. B 2013.
- African elephant brain: 257 billion neurons, 97.5% in the cerebellum, 5.6 billion in the cortex vs ~16 billion in humans: Herculano-Houzel et al., Frontiers in Neuroanatomy 2014. Mirror self-recognition: Plotnik, de Waal & Reiss, PNAS 2006.
- Ayumu's memory test: Inoue & Matsuzawa, Current Biology 2007 (later studies show practised humans can match him).
- Human brain ~86 billion neurons: Azevedo et al. 2009. Oldest Homo sapiens fossils ~300,000 years: Hublin et al., Nature 2017.
