You text a friend in New York. You'd think it bounces off a satellite. It doesn't. It goes under the ocean. Your phone sends it by radio to the nearest tower. From there it turns into light, racing through glass fibers under the streets to the coast. In a plain little building by the beach, it enters a cable that slides into the sea. On the ocean floor, often four kilometers down, lies a cable about as thick as a garden hose. Inside, glass threads as thin as a hair carry your words as flashes of light. Every fifty kilometers or so, a repeater boosts the light, powered by thousands of volts sent from shore. Sharks have bitten these cables, but most breaks come from ship anchors and fishing nets. Five and a half thousand kilometers later, it comes ashore near New York, and your friend's phone buzzes. The whole ocean took less than a twentieth of a second.
