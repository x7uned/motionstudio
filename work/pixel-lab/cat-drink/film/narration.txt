Your cat drinks without scooping a single drop. Its tongue barely touches the water, four times a second, too fast to see. So scientists at MIT filmed a cat named Cutta Cutta with a high-speed camera. Here's what they saw. The tip of the tongue curls back, and only its top touches the water. Then the cat pulls it up, fast. Water sticks to the tongue and rises with it in a thin column. But gravity pulls that column back down. So the cat shuts its mouth at just the right moment, right before the water falls. Too late, and the tongue comes back empty. Lions and tigers use the same trick, only slower, because their tongues are bigger. Dogs lift water the same way, but they hit it much harder. That's why dogs splash so much.
