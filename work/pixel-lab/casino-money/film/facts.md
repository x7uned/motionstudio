# Film 4 — How the casino makes money feel fake (research, checked 2026-10-27)

Method: web search + page fetches. Not read directly: Raghubir paper full text, Harrah's primary sources, Lapuz & Griffiths results.
Verdicts: TRUE / PARTLY / MYTH / DEBATED. Mostly US (Las Vegas) unless noted.

## Claims

1. **Chips make money feel less real ("monopoly money")** — PARTLY. General effect is TRUE: Raghubir & Srivastava (2008), *J. Experimental Psychology: Applied* 14(3):213–225: with the same face value, people spent more with scrip/gift certificates than cash (credit-card logos also raised spending); effect shrank when the act of paying was made vivid. That was shopping, NOT casino chips. For gambling the proof is thin: Palmer, Cringle & Clark (2022), *Int. Gambling Studies* 22(3):499–521, a scoping review of 19 papers/23 experiments, found few studies and "mixed" results for cash vs other formats; one lab slot study (Limbrick-Oldfield et al.) found no difference in bet size across cash/voucher. Lapuz & Griffiths (2010), "The role of chips in poker gambling: an empirical pilot study", *Gambling Research* 22(1):34–39 exists but I could not read its result. So: "chips feel less like money" = plausible and widely taught; "chips make you bet more" = not firmly shown.
   - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18808275/
   - https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/intgms/v22y2022i3p499-521.html
   - https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/13475
   - Chip colours: the US set (white $1, red $5, green $25, black $100) is a custom; I did not verify a source this session, skip in the film.

2. **Player cards track every bet; casino computes "theoretical loss" (theo)** — TRUE. Theo = average bet x bets per hour x hours x house edge. Example: $25 blackjack, 50 hands/h, 4 h = $5,000 in action, 2% edge = $100 expected loss. Harrah's Total Rewards (card used by ~80% of its patrons, 44 million customers, per 2008 case study) is the best-known system; it showed that repeat mid-stakes slot players, not whales, made most profit.
   - https://www.lasvegasadvisor.com/question/2005-10-17/
   - https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/casino-comps-theoretical-loss-how-rewards-work
   - https://saylordotorg.github.io/text_information-systems-a-managers-guide-to-harnessing-technology-v1.0/s15-08-data-asset-in-action-harrah-s-.html

3. **Comps are a percentage of expected loss** — TRUE, range varies. Sources say 10–40% (one: 20–40%; Las Vegas Advisor 2005: 25–40% of theo or 10% of actual loss, whichever greater). Depends on competition and game. Do NOT say "30%" as a fixed rule.
   - https://www.lasvegasadvisor.com/question/2005-10-17/
   - https://insideflyer.com/2015/10/10/what-is-a-casino-comp/

4. **"Pain point": casino steps in when you are about to be unhappy** — DEBATED / thinly sourced. A 2004 trade article (East Chicago test) says staff approached players on losing streaks with a free meal; a case study says Harrah's tracks "unusual losses" and sends a host with a buffet coupon. The named "pain point" threshold model and "free steak dinner" come from one 2008 blog summary of Ian Ayres' *Super Crunchers*; no Harrah's primary source found. Say "casinos use data to react when a guest is losing", not "Harrah's calculates your pain point".
   - https://michaeljamesonmoney.com/2008/09/cost-of-free-dinner.html
   - https://www.cioinsight.com/news-trends/make-every-customer-more-profitable-harrahs-entertainment-inc
   - https://chiefmarketer.com/harrahs-woos-gamblers-on-the-floor

5. **Free drinks lower caution / keep you playing** — PARTLY. Lab results are mixed. Kyngdon & Dickerson (1999), *Addiction* 94:697–707: moderate-to-heavy male drinkers on a losing schedule kept playing until their stake was gone far more often than placebo (about 50% vs 15%), but baseline impulsivity differed and average bet did not change. Ellery, Stewart & Loba (2005): moderate alcohol dose -> longer play on a video lottery terminal (clearest in probable pathological gamblers). Cronce & Corbin (2010), *Exp. Clin. Psychopharm.* 18(2):145–157, 130 young adults: alcohol -> larger bets and faster loss of funds, NOT more persistence. Sagoe et al. (2017), 184 people: no effect. Small lab samples.
   - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3039524/
   - https://latam-cl.demo.elsevierpure.com/es/publications/alcohol-affects-video-lottery-terminal-vlt-gambling-behaviors-and

6. **Free drinks rules by country** — TRUE, varies. Las Vegas Strip: complimentary drinks to people playing slots/tables are normal; rule says no comped drinks for visibly impaired guests, rarely enforced (Las Vegas Sun 2006; casino.org). In 2008 free liquor was only ~36.9% of bar sales in 23 big Strip casinos. Australia (NSW): illegal to give free or discounted alcohol to induce gambling on gaming machines; operator ALH fined ~$172,000 plus 2-week machine ban. UK: 2005 report said the Gambling Act would stop casinos giving free drinks/food/rooms as inducement; I could not confirm exact casino licence wording, and UK adult gaming centres may not allow alcohol while gambling is offered. Atlantic City/New Jersey: not verified.
   - https://lasvegassun.com/news/2006/jan/09/on-the-strip-drinking-not-free-for-all/
   - https://pubtic.com.au/alh-cops-heavy-penalties-for-nsw-gaming-breaches/
   - https://morningadvertiser.co.uk/Article/2005/03/08/Casino-ban-on-free-food-and-drink-welcome-news-to-pubs

7. **Slot "credits" hide dollars** — PARTLY. Machines show credits/lines; players underestimate losses (Australian Productivity Commission figure quoted by advocacy site: those who say they spent $100 had spent ~$3,448 on average; secondary source). Harrigan (Waterloo) lists "currency not displayed" among features tied to faulty beliefs (slide deck, not peer review). No clean credits-vs-dollars experiment found. Safer claim: modern slots show wins and credits, and "losses disguised as wins" make net loss hard to feel (Dixon et al. 2010 *Addiction*).
   - https://www.stoppredatorygambling.org/problem-gamblers-cant-judge-the-cost-of-electronic-gambling-machines/
   - https://uwaterloo.ca/news/news/music-gamblers-ears

8. **ATMs in casinos and bans** — PARTLY. TRUE: Victoria (Australia) removed ATMs from pokies venues from July 2012 (EFTPOS stayed). Swinburne evaluation: pokies spending down ~7%; problem gamblers spent about $90 less per visit in hotels, $43 in clubs; most entrenched players found outside ATMs; one local-data report disputes the trend. Tasmania reportedly banned ATMs in gaming venues; Victoria/SA/NT capped withdrawals at $200 per card per transaction. Las Vegas floor ATM fees: no solid source found (only a 2009 blog). UK: 17% of ATMs in gambling premises were in casinos (Bristol report, 2020) — no ban found.
   - https://www.aph.gov.au/About_Parliament/Parliamentary_departments/Parliamentary_Library/FlagPost/2013/September/Addressing_harms_from_pokies_insights_from_new_reports
   - https://assets.pc.gov.au/inquiries/completed/gambling-2010/submissions/subdr381.pdf
   - https://bristol.ac.uk/media-library/sites/geography/pfrc/[164318]-[CD]-Report - cash and gambling.pdf

9. **Whales get a % of losses back, free flights, limos** — PARTLY (industry practice, old/anecdotal sources). Loss rebate: casino refunds a share (often quoted 10–20%, sometimes more) of a high roller's losses; origin in 1970s unpaid debts. Hosts arrange jets, cars, villas (Steve Cyr, *Whale Hunt in the Desert*; Arnold Snyder, 2002, Las Vegas Advisor). Academic note: Lucas et al. (2002) found high rollers don't automatically mean high profit. No named modern press on specific casino programs.
   - https://www.pokernews.com/casino/casino-terms/high-roller.htm
   - https://www.lasvegasadvisor.com/gambling-with-an-edge/?p=123920
   - https://resident.com/resource-guide/2025/06/17/inside-the-elite-world-of-casino-whales

## Extra facts
- **RFID in chips**: Wynn Las Vegas (2005) chips carry a serial-number chip; antennas follow chips from cage to tables and flag fakes; some tables read the bet. TRUE for some casinos, not all. https://www.tampabay.com/archive/2005/06/20/wired-chips-track-high-rollers/ , https://informationweek.com/it-leadership/casinos-gamble-on-rfid-gaming-chips
- **Dealer clothes have no pockets / small apron** — anti-theft. TRUE. https://www.npr.org/transcripts/246809717
- **Slot wins/losses**: the machine can play a win sound when you got back less than you bet (Dixon 2010/2013). TRUE.
- **Heavy repeat slot players, not whales, drive most profit at Harrah's**: >30% of customers (spending $100–$500/visit) gave >80% of revenue and nearly 100% of profit (case study). TRUE per source.
- Not verified, do not state: "chips worthless outside the casino" (true for most chips in practice, no source fetched), "markers" explanation, specific whale jet policies.

## Myths to avoid
- "Chips make you bet 100% more" — the 100% number comes from credit-card studies, not chips.
- "Casinos calculate your exact pain point and send a steak" — one blog; keep general.
- "Comps are always 30%" — range 10–40%.
- "Alcohol makes everyone gamble longer" — mixed lab results.
- "Free drinks everywhere" — banned/limited in places like NSW.
- "Credits prove players lose track" — plausible, not directly tested.

## Best 6 facts for a simple 55-second film
1. Your player card records every bet, and the casino multiplies bet size, speed, time and the game's edge to guess how much you will lose.
2. Free food, rooms and shows are paid as a share of that guess — usually 10 to 40 percent of the expected loss.
3. Chips and gift cards feel less like money: in shopping tests people spent more with scrip than with cash of the same value (Raghubir 2008).
4. Free drinks on the floor are normal in Las Vegas, but in New South Wales, Australia, giving free drinks to people on slot machines is illegal.
5. When Victoria, Australia, removed ATMs from slot venues in 2012, slot spending fell about 7 percent.
6. Wynn's casino chips carry RFID tags, so the casino can follow each chip and spot fakes.
