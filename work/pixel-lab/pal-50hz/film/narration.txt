If you grew up in Europe, your video games really did run slower. About seventeen percent slower. Here's why. Old TVs drew the picture in time with the electricity in the wall. In America and Japan, that's sixty times a second. In Europe, it's fifty. Many games moved everything one step per picture. So in Europe, they got fifty steps a second instead of sixty. Take Sonic the Hedgehog, in 1991. On a European console, Sonic ran slower, the music played slower, and even the game's clock ticked slower. So your best time on the screen took longer in real life. And the picture was made for a smaller screen, so black bars filled the rest. Most kids never knew. There was nothing faster to compare it with. Some games were fixed for Europe, but many weren't. Some players even opened their consoles and added a sixty switch. The first console to really fix it was the Dreamcast, in 1999. Follow, so you don't miss the next one.
