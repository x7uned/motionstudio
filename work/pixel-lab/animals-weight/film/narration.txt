More than a billion honeybees weigh as much as one blue whale. So let's climb from light to heavy. A honeybee weighs a tenth of a gram. Yet it flies home with nectar almost half as heavy as itself. The bee hummingbird weighs less than two grams, so people often think it's a bee. Scientists think a bird can't get much lighter: its body would lose heat too fast. A mouse weighs about twenty grams. It's so light that air slows its fall. A famous scientist wrote that a mouse could drop down a deep mine and walk away. A cat is two hundred times heavier, so falls hurt more. But it twists in the air and lands on its feet. A dog weighs about twenty kilograms. Yet all the dogs on Earth weigh as much as all the wild land mammals. And all people together weigh about twenty times more than that. A horse weighs half a tonne, and it stands on just one toe on each leg. A hippo is heavier still. It can't swim, and it can't even float. So it walks along the river bottom. The elephant is the heaviest land animal, about six tonnes. It eats up to a hundred and fifty kilograms of plants a day. But a blue whale weighs as much as twenty-five elephants. Its heart alone weighs as much as three people. So how many bees would it take to balance this giant?
