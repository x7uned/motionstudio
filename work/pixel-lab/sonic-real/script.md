## Narration (EN, read word for word), 232 words, ≈74–77 s

> In our world, Usain Bolt would beat Sonic in a race. Let's bring him here and check. Sonic's official profile says he is about one meter tall. In the first Sonic game, he is 39 pixels tall, so one pixel is about two and a half centimeters. His top running speed is 6 pixels per frame, at 60 frames a second. That's 33 kilometers an hour. Usain Bolt's top speed is almost 45. But that's not the Sonic from the screen. In the movie, a police radar clocks him at about 300 miles an hour. That's about 480 kilometers an hour. In cartoons and comics, he runs faster than sound, over 1,200 kilometers an hour. That's where his name comes from. So how does the game Sonic feel so fast? He rolls. Curled into a ball and rolling down a hill, he can reach 16 pixels per frame. That's 88 kilometers an hour, as fast as a car on a highway. Now add a loop as tall as a two-story house. You need about 44 kilometers an hour at the bottom, or you fall off the ceiling. Running, Sonic would fall. Rolling, he makes it easily. But a rolling ball can't speed itself up. On flat ground, it only slows down. So Sonic's real superpower isn't his legs. It's hills. Next, a Creeper comes to our world. Follow, so you don't miss it.

Calculations (scale 39 px = 1 m → 1 px = 2.56 cm):
- running: 6 × 60 × 0.0256 = 9.2 m/s = **33 km/h**;
- rolling: 16 × 60 × 0.0256 = 24.6 m/s = **88.6 km/h**; 88.6 / 44.7 = **1.98** («twice as fast as Bolt»);
- loop 6 m tall (r = 3 m), with no friction: at the top you need v² ≥ g·r, so at the bottom v ≥ √(5·g·r) = 12.1 m/s = **43.7 km/h** («about 44»);
- at 33 km/h, Sonic does not even reach the top: v² = 85 − 2·9.81·6 < 0;
- at 88.6 km/h, the speed at the top is ≈22 m/s, against the 5.4 m/s needed: «with speed to spare».

Assumptions:
- The 6 m loop height («as tall as a two-story house») is our choice, not the size of a loop from the game. The text does not say that game loops are that size.
- Friction and the size of the ball are ignored in the loop calculation.
