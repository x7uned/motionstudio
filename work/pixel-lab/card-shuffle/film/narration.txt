Shuffle a deck well, and you'll hold an order no one has ever held. Not in any casino, not in any game, ever. The first card can be any of fifty-two. The next, any of fifty-one. Multiply all the way down, and you get a number with sixty-eight digits. Let every person on Earth shuffle once a second since the Big Bang. The chance that any two of those decks ever matched is about one in thirteen trillion. But only if you shuffle well. After one riffle, a new deck still keeps much of its old order. In 1992, two mathematicians showed you need about seven riffles. The slow overhand shuffle needs thousands. And a perfect shuffle isn't random at all. Cut the deck in exact halves, then weave one card from each side. Do it eight times, and every card is back where it started. Magicians and card cheats use it on purpose.
